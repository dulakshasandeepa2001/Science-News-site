BMI introduction
BMI is a measurement of a person's leanness or corpulence based on their height and weight, and is intended to quantify tissue mass. It is widely used as a general indicator of whether a person has a healthy body weight for their height. Specifically, the value obtained from the calculation of BMI is used to categorize whether a person is underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obese depending on what range the value falls between. These ranges of BMI vary based on factors such as region and age, and are sometimes further divided into subcategories such as severely underweight or very severely obese. Being overweight or underweight can have significant health effects, so while BMI is an imperfect measure of healthy body weight, it is a useful indicator of whether any additional testing or action is required. Risks associated with being overweight
Being overweight increases the risk of a number of serious diseases and health conditions. Below is a list of said risks, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC):

High blood pressure
Higher levels of LDL cholesterol, which is widely considered "bad cholesterol," lower levels of HDL cholesterol, considered to be good cholesterol in moderation, and high levels of triglycerides
Type II diabetes
Coronary heart disease
Stroke
Gallbladder disease
Osteoarthritis, a type of joint disease caused by breakdown of joint cartilage
Sleep apnea and breathing problems
Certain cancers (endometrial, breast, colon, kidney, gallbladder, liver)
Low quality of life
Mental illnesses such as clinical depression, anxiety, and others
Body pains and difficulty with certain physical functions
Generally, an increased risk of mortality compared to those with a healthy BMI
As can be seen from the list above, there are numerous negative, in some cases fatal, outcomes that may result from being overweight. Generally, a person should try to maintain a BMI below 25 kg/m2, but ideally should consult their doctor to determine whether or not they need to make any changes to their lifestyle in order to be healthier.

Risks associated with being underweight
Being underweight has its own associated risks, listed below:

Malnutrition, vitamin deficiencies, anemia (lowered ability to carry blood vessels)
Osteoporosis, a disease that causes bone weakness, increasing the risk of breaking a bone
A decrease in immune function
Growth and development issues, particularly in children and teenagers
Possible reproductive issues for women due to hormonal imbalances that can disrupt the menstrual cycle. Underweight women also have a higher chance of miscarriage in the first trimester
Potential complications as a result of surgery
Generally, an increased risk of mortality compared to those with a healthy BMI
In some cases, being underweight can be a sign of some underlying condition or disease such as anorexia nervosa, which has its own risks. Consult your doctor if you think you or someone you know is underweight, particularly if the reason for being underweight does not seem obvious.

Limitations of BMI
Although BMI is a widely used and useful indicator of healthy body weight, it does have its limitations. BMI is only an estimate that cannot take body composition into account. Due to a wide variety of body types as well as distribution of muscle, bone mass, and fat, BMI should be considered along with other measurements rather than being used as the sole method for determining a person's healthy body weight.

In adults:

BMI cannot be fully accurate because it is a measure of excess body weight, rather than excess body fat. BMI is further influenced by factors such as age, sex, ethnicity, muscle mass, body fat, and activity level, among others. For example, an older person who is considered a healthy weight, but is completely inactive in their daily life may have significant amounts of excess body fat even though they are not heavy. This would be considered unhealthy, while a younger person with higher muscle composition of the same BMI would be considered healthy. In athletes, particularly bodybuilders who would be considered overweight due to muscle being heavier than fat, it is entirely possible that they are actually at a healthy weight for their body composition. Generally, according to the CDC:

Older adults tend to have more body fat than younger adults with the same BMI.
Women tend to have more body fat than men for an equivalent BMI.
Muscular individuals and highly trained athletes may have higher BMIs due to large muscle mass.
In children and adolescents:

The same factors that limit the efficacy of BMI for adults can also apply to children and adolescents. Additionally, height and level of sexual maturation can influence BMI and body fat among children. BMI is a better indicator of excess body fat for obese children than it is for overweight children, whose BMI could be a result of increased levels of either fat or fat-free mass (all body components except for fat, which includes water, organs, muscle, etc.). In thin children, the difference in BMI can also be due to fat-free mass.

That being said, BMI is fairly indicative of body fat for 90-95% of the population, and can effectively be used along with other measures to help determine an individual's healthy body weight.

Ponderal Index
The Ponderal Index (PI) is similar to BMI in that it measures the leanness or corpulence of a person based on their height and weight. The main difference between the PI and BMI is the cubing rather than squaring of the height in the formula (provided below). While BMI can be a useful tool when considering large populations, it is not reliable for determining leanness or corpulence in individuals. Although the PI suffers from similar considerations, the PI is more reliable for use with very tall or short individuals, while BMI tends to record uncharacteristically high or low body fat levels for those on the extreme ends of the height and weight spectrum. Mayo Clinic BMI and waist circumference calculator
BMI uses weight and height to estimate body fat. A high BMI is associated with an increased risk of chronic diseases such as heart disease, high blood pressure, and type 2 diabetes.

BMI provides a reasonable estimate of body fat for most people. However, it has limitations. For example, BMI may:

Underestimate body fat for older adults or people with low muscle mass
Overestimate body fat for people who are especially muscular
In addition, BMI may underestimate risk for people of Asian descent, in part because it doesn't factor in the risk associated with excess abdominal fat, (which is slightly greater among southeast Asians).

Therefore, it's useful to look at waist size (measured slightly above the hipbones in adults) in addition to BMI. Health risks increase as waist size increases, regardless of BMI.

Talk with your doctor if you have questions about your BMI or waist size.

What is BMI?
Body Mass Index or BMI is an estimate of a person's leanness or body fat based on their body weight and height, and is designed to assess tissue mass. It is commonly used as a general measure of whether a person's body weight is appropriate for their height.

Specifically, the result derived from the BMI calculation is used to determine whether a person is underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obese, depending on which category the value falls into.

These BMI values vary depending on geography and age, and are often further classified as extremely underweight or very severely obese.

Although BMI is not a perfect estimate of healthy body weight, it may be a helpful tool in determining if further testing or treatment is necessary because being overweight or underweight can have serious health consequences.

What is a BMI Calculator?
The Body Mass Index calculator or BMI calculator is a useful tool that determines your current weight—whether you are overweight, underweight, or just adequate. That said, a persons weight cannot be the sole indicator of their health. A person with a 5.10 ft. height can easily weigh more than one with a 5.5 ft. height.

A BMI calculator takes into account both - the height and weight, to give a better idea. BMI also takes into account geological as well as age factors while calculating. When analyzing a customers application for life insurance and health insurance, BMI is taken into account to understand their health condition to propose an appropriate premium amount.

How Is BMI Calculated?
A step-by-step calculation of body mass index requires accurate measurement of height and weight. After correctly measuring them, follow the given steps:

Step 1: Multiply your height (H) by itself (H × H).
Step 2: Divide your weight (W) by the answer derived in the first step. (W/H2)
The formula to calculate BMI is as follows:

1. International System of Units
In metric unit below is the formula to calculate BMI

BMI = weight (kg)/ height² (m²)

For example, if a man is 1.5 meters tall and weighs 72 kg, then his:

BMI = 72/ (1.5)² = 72 / 2.25 = 32

2. Imperial System
BMI = 703 [weight (lbs) /height² (inches²)

For example, if a woman is 5’2” (62 inches) and weighs 145 pounds, then her BMI would be:

BMI = 703 [145/ (62×62)] = 703 X [145/3844] = 26.5

Note: To avoid unnecessary human errors, you should use an Online BMI calculator for accurate results.

How to Use the BMI Calculator?
Here are the steps to calculate BMI:

Enter Your Weight & Height
Begin by recording your height and weight as accurately as possible for reliable results.
Square Your Height
Convert height into metres or inches, then square the value (height × height).
Apply the BMI Formula
Divide your weight by your squared height. If using pounds/inches, multiply the outcome by 703.
Calculate Your BMI Value
Use a BMI calculator to confirm your result instantly and compare it with standard BMI categories.

Why Use a BMI Calculator?
The BMI calculator is not a tool in trend but a necessity these days since the obesity rates globally are on its all time high. It is used to analyse the following key health aspects:

1. Monitor Body Fat
Monitoring body fat is essential to understand the risks associated with being overweight and underweight. Additionally, the right amount of body fat helps the body utilise vitamins and minerals, providing optimal energy.
2. Prevent Health Conditions Later in Life
Healthcare professionals use a body mass index calculator to diagnose various lifestyle related health issues associated to weight of individuals. It classifies weight types by BMI ranges, helping to determine individuals as overweight or underweight.
3. Weight Management
Calculating your actual weight through BMI helps you set realistic goals for a healthier weight. You can set an effective weight management strategy to balance your weight if you are within the overweight or underweight range.
4. Height-to-Weight Ratio
The body mass index calculator helps you analyse your weight in accordance with your height. Individuals' weights are not constant within an age group. Hence, BMI accounts for height to provide more accurate weight estimates.
5. Early Detection of Serious Health Risks
Studies and researches in last 10 years suggest a strong link between a High BMI and certain types of cancers and type 2 diabetes. Since prevention is cure, BMI calculator helps in keeping the track of your weight and maintaining it within limits.

Importance of a BMI Calculator
BMI calculator plays a vital role to check whether a person is healthy or not. It is an easy to use tool for assessing the health of an individual and also works as the ‘Obesity Indicator. Following reasons makes BMI calculator a useful tool for public:

Assessing body weight and related health risks: By using a BMI calculator, a physician can get an idea of the patient's current health status. Based on this, they can come up with a better treatment plan. Also, if there are signs of potential health issues like high blood pressure or diabetes, the patient can be informed and advised accordingly.
Managing body weight: On an individual level, the BMI calculator works as an ‘Obesity Indicator,’ since it is useful in managing how much weight one should lose or gain to be in the normal BMI range. If used periodically, it can track changes and make amends, if needed.
Public health assessment: To prepare national health-related data, BMI plays a significant role. For this purpose, BMI calculators are particularly useful.
To determine insurance premium: When determining the life insurance premium (or health insurance premium), insurance companies often ask for medical tests or conduct the same themselves. Weight Tracking: BMI calculators are very beneficial for weight watchers during their weight loss or gain journey. The tools help in periodic tracking and keep the weight goals in check.

Is BMI Reliable?
The body mass index calculator is an essential tool for checking weight and assessing certain health conditions. Although it can be used as a general health indicator, it cannot be used as a diagnostic tool.

Healthcare providers use BMI alongside several other indicators such as blood tests, screening, imaging etc. to accurately rule out any health issues.

Furthermore, health conditions depend on genetics and other environmental factors in addition to body mass index. Hence, it is essential to visit a professional healthcare provider to obtain an accurate diagnosis and not to rely solely on BMI results.

Limitations of an Online BMI Calculator
While BMI is a standard indicator of ones health status, especially in the field of health insurance and life insurance in India, it isnt a fool-proof system. While many BMI calculators do ask for ones gender and age along with their height and weight, but due to a lack of differential BMI ranges, the BMI result dont reveal the exact picture.

Lets understand the limitations of BMI for adults, children and teenagers as explained below:

No Male-Female Division:
A common disadvantage of BMI is that it doesn't take into account whether the person in question is a male or a female. Its basic science that males and females have different levels of body fat necessary for optimum condition of their physical and mental working.

No Difference Between Muscle and Fat
When calculating BMI, ones height and weight are considered. It cannot distinguish between the body fat and body muscle leading to not-so-correct calculations. A person who does weight training and has a high-protein diet is going to have more muscles than fat. But BMI calculation cannot cut down the muscle-content from fat resulting in a high BMI of that person.

BMI limitation for Children
A childs body is on an ongoing curve. Their BMI can fluctuate significantly because of continuing changes in their height as well as weight. An active child falling low on BMI but it will show them to be underweight, giving a false indication of poor health.

Apart from these, BMI does not consider the role played by ones ethnicity and race on their height and weight. Asians generally have up to 5% more body fat than Caucasians. Similarly, Africans and Afro-Americans touch the height bars higher as compared to Asians and Caucasians. BMI does not take into account these facts.

The risk of diabetes is highest in Asians, followed by African-Americans and Caucasians. This is totally disregarded in the BMI calculations, thus giving half-baked health indicators.

Although the body mass index calculator is an essential tool to calculate body fat, it comes with certain limitations:

Limitations to Diagnose Weight Types	Limitations to Assess Health Conditions
Body mass index does not differentiate between fat mass and lean body mass. Lean body mass refers to the weight of everything in your body other than fat.
A person can have a high BMI if they are muscular but have a very low fat mass. It leads to inaccurate results.
Females have more body fat than males. So, a standard BMI chart cannot be used for both genders.
The body mass index chart is not adjusted for the increasing average height of adults over the years.
Body mass index is not an accurate measure for athletes, bodybuilders, children, pregnant women, and senior citizens.
The body mass index calculator does not account for the location and distribution of fat in the body.
Excess fat in certain areas of the body, such as the abdomen, can lead to greater health risks than excess fat in other areas, such as the thighs.
The relationship between the rate of death and body mass index does not account for factors such as family history of diseases, including diabetes and cancer, or familial lifespan.
Certain health conditions can result from other factors, such as the environment and genetics.
Inaccurate or False positive body mass index results can lead to unnecessary health-related stress.
Alternate Estimates of Weight-Related Health Risks
Body mass index is not always reliable or accurate. Here are some alternatives for measuring weight-related health risks:

1. Waist Circumference
Waist circumference measures the amount of fat deposited in the abdomen. It can be determined using a measuring tape.

A waist circumference of 35 inches and above in women (34 inches for Asian women) and 40 inches and above in men (36 inches for Asian men) indicates higher abdominal fat. Although it is an easy-to-measure method, there is no standardisation of measurement.
2. Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR)
Waist-to-hip ratio is an essential tool to measure abdominal obesity. It can be calculated using the waist circumference and the hip circumference at its widest diameter. Then, the waist measurement is divided by the hip measurement to obtain the accurate waist-to-hip ratio.

A waist-to-hip ratio of 0.86 or higher in women and 1.0 or higher in men indicates high health risks. Meanwhile, a waist-to-hip ratio of 0.80 or lower in women and 0.95 or lower in men indicates a lower risk of health issues.
3. Skinfold Thickness
Skinfold thickness is an alternative measure that provides an analysis of fat beneath the skin at different body sites and estimates total body fat.

It is measured using a special calliper by folding and squeezing the skin and placing the calliper at various locations, such as the trunk, thighs, and back. Then, you need to enter the values derived by the calliper at different online calculators to get the final result.

What Is Ponderal Index and How Is It Calculated?
Ponderal Index (PI), also known as the Rohrer’s Index, is a measure that relates a person's weight to their height. It is similar to BMI but uses height raised to the third power in the calculation.

The ponderal index relates weight to height cubed. The formula's numerical scaling and units depend on whether measurements are entered in kilograms and metres or grams and centimetres.

Ponderal Index Formula for Adults
For adults, the Ponderal Index can be calculated using weight in kilograms and height in metres:

PI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)³

The resulting value is generally expressed in kg/m³.
Ponderal Index Formula for Infants
For infants, PI is commonly calculated using weight in grams and length in centimetres:

PI = Weight (g) ÷ Length (cm)³ × 100

The resulting value is generally expressed in g/cm³.
Ponderal Index Ranges
Ponderal Index interpretation can vary depending on age, measurement units and the reference used. The following table can be used as a simple reference where the applicable PI ranges are provided by the relevant calculator or source.
Group	PI Formula	Commonly Used Unit	Interpretation
Adults	Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)³	kg/m³	Used to assess body size in relation to height
Infants	Weight (g) ÷ Length (cm)³ × 100	g/cm³	Used to assess body proportionality during early growth


PI should not be treated as a standalone measure of health. For infants and growing children, healthcare professionals may use age-specific growth measurements and other clinical factors for assessment.
Ponderal Index Formula
The Ponderal Index (PI) formula helps measure the leanness of a human body by dividing your weight by the cube of your height. For children and adolescents, this formula provides a more accurate calculation than a body mass index calculator, as it can capture the changes in body composition due to growth. PI helps classify weight status and assess health risks more precisely in growing individuals.

The PI formula for infants and adults is provided below:

PI formula for infants = (Birth weight (g) × 100) ÷ (Crown heel length (cm))3

PI formula for adults = Ponderal index = Weight (Kg) ÷ (Height (m))3

The ideal PI range for infants is between 2.2 and 3. However, the range for adults is divided into the following categories:

Does BMI Affect Your Health Insurance Premium?
BMI has a crucial role in determining your health insurance premium. Your Body Mass Index (BMI) usually indicates your health condition. Thus, health insurance companies often use a BMI calculator to assess your health risk by simply looking at your BMI.

As a result, the BMI of a policyholder influences their health insurance premium in the following ways:

Individuals with Higher BMI
If you have a higher BMI above 24.9, you may develop obesity-related conditions such as diabetes, cardiac issues, hypertension, etc. All these conditions increase the chances of frequent insurance claims. Thus, insurance companies charge a higher premium for those with higher BMIs, considering their health risks.

Individuals with Lower BMI
If your BMI is below 18.5, then you have a lower Body Mass Index. A lower BMI also increases the risk of malnutrition-related health issues. Thus, insurance companies charge higher premiums for such policyholders because they face a higher risk of health issues.

Individuals with Healthy BMI
Those with a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 are considered healthy. Such individuals can negotiate with the insurance company for lower premiums or discounts on health insurance policies.

Having low health risks means their frequency of health insurance claims will be lower. So, the insurance company considers them to have a low-risk profile and charges a lower premium than those with higher BMI.

Knowing how BMI affects your health insurance premium underscores the importance of maintaining a healthy BMI for both financial and health reasons. It also highlights the importance of BMI in healthcare and its impact on policyholders, healthcare providers, and insurers.
