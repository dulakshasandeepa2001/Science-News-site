  - **Answer**: No. While temperatures in London and southern England are forecast to reach 24°C to 25°C, the warm spell will not last the required three consecutive days above regional threshold limits (which is 28°C for London).

- **Question**: What is the UK heatwave threshold?
  - **Answer**: The Met Office defines a heatwave as a period of at least three consecutive days where daily maximum temperatures meet or exceed a specific threshold, ranging from 25°C in Scotland, Northern Ireland, and northern England to 28°C in Greater London.

- **Question**: How hot was summer 2026 in the UK?
  - **Answer**: Summer 2026 was officially verified by the Met Office as the hottest summer in UK recorded history, registering an overall mean temperature of 16.5°C across June, July, and August.

- **Question**: When will temperatures drop back down?
  - **Answer**: The unseasonable warmth will peak on Monday before cooler Atlantic air masses, scattered showers, and brisk westerly winds return from Tuesday onward, bringing highs back to seasonal autumn norms of 16°C to 18°C.

- **Question**: Why does the UK feel unusually hot at 25°C compared to other countries?
  - **Answer**: The UK's high oceanic humidity, maritime climate, and domestic architectural design (optimized for heat retention and insulation rather than active cross-ventilation or air conditioning) make mid-20s temperatures feel significantly warmer and more oppressive.
