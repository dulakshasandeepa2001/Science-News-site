## 5. Structured FAQ Summary

- **Question**: What is a super-Earth?
  - **Answer**: A super-Earth is an exoplanet with a mass higher than Earth's but significantly lower than our solar system's ice giants (Uranus and Neptune), typically ranging between 2 and 10 times Earth's mass.

- **Question**: Did the Sun actually swallow a planet?
  - **Answer**: Astrophysical computer modeling from Ege University demonstrates that the Sun absorbing a 5-to-10 Earth-mass super-Earth billions of years ago accurately reproduces the Sun’s current internal sound-speed structure and depleted lithium levels, making it a strong scientific candidate.

- **Question**: Why doesn’t our solar system have a super-Earth today?
  - **Answer**: Super-Earths are extremely common around other stars in the galaxy. If this study is confirmed, our solar system may have originally formed one, but orbital decay forced it into the young Sun during the early formation of the planetary disk.

- **Question**: How do scientists detect remnants of a swallowed planet inside the Sun?
  - **Answer**: Through helioseismology—measuring acoustic sound waves and pressure oscillations traveling through the Sun's interior using space observatories like NASA's SDO and ESA's Solar Orbiter.

- **Question**: Is our Sun in danger of swallowing any remaining planets like Earth?
  - **Answer**: No. The planets in our modern solar system occupy stable, locked orbits with negligible gas drag. The Sun will not engulf inner planets until its red giant expansion phase roughly 5 billion years from now.
