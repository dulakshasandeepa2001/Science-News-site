## 5. Structured FAQ Summary

- **Question**: Why did Rocket Lab protest NASA's Mars orbiter contract?
  - **Answer**: Rocket Lab filed a formal protest with the GAO arguing that NASA’s $700 million award to Blue Origin violated statutory eligibility criteria mandated by Congress in the 2025 appropriations bill and that NASA conducted an inconsistent, punitive review of Rocket Lab's technical proposal.

- **Question**: What is the Mars Telecommunications Network (MTN)?
  - **Answer**: The MTN is a NASA mission to develop and deploy a dedicated, high-bandwidth commercial communications relay satellite in Mars orbit by 2030, replacing the aging 2001 Mars Odyssey and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) spacecraft.

- **Question**: What spacecraft is Blue Origin using for the Mars mission?
  - **Answer**: Blue Origin proposed a specialized derivative of its Blue Ring spacecraft bus, featuring hybrid chemical and solar-electric propulsion and 13 external payload attachment ports.

- **Question**: What happens if the GAO upholds Rocket Lab’s protest?
  - **Answer**: If the GAO sustains the protest, it will typically recommend that NASA re-open the procurement, re-evaluate competing technical proposals from Rocket Lab and Blue Origin, or issue a revised contract solicitation.

- **Question**: Why is the 2028 Mars launch window so critical?
  - **Answer**: Earth and Mars align for optimal orbital transfer trajectories only once every 26 months. Missing the late-2028 launch window would delay the mission to 2031, leaving NASA's surface rovers vulnerable to communication blackouts as legacy orbiters age.
