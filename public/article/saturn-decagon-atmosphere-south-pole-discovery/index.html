Astronomers Detect a Giant 10-Sided 'Decagon' in Saturn's Atmosphere

NASA's Hubble Space Telescope has detected a colossal 10-sided atmospheric wave—a 'Decagon'—spanning over 104,000 miles around Saturn's south pole, confirming polygon jet streams can form in both planetary hemispheres.

✍️ By: Hubble & Planetary Astrophysics Editorial Desk 📅 Published: September 4, 2026 ⏱️ Read Time: 6 min read

1.A Second Geometric Marvel in the Ringed Planet's Skies

For more than four decades, Saturn has held the title of the Solar System’s geometric anomaly. Ever since NASA’s Voyager spacecraft flew past the gas giant in 1980 and 1981, scientists have marveled over the massive, six-sided atmospheric vortex churning perpetually at its north pole: the famous Saturnian Hexagon.

Now, planetary scientists have confirmed that the hexagon is no longer a solitary oddity.

Orbiting high above Earth, NASA’s Hubble Space Telescope has detected a gigantic, newly emerging 10-sided atmospheric wave—a "Decagon"—encircling Saturn’s south pole. The breakthrough discovery, published in Science Advances by an international team led by Professor Agustín Sánchez-Lavega of the University of the Basque Country (UPV/EHU), confirms that polygon-shaped jet streams can form in both polar hemispheres of the ringed planet.

2.The Numbers Behind the Monster: 100,000 Miles Across

While the northern hexagon spans an impressive 20,000 miles (32,000 kilometers) across, the newly confirmed southern decagon operates on an even grander scale:

• Total Width: Approximately 104,250 miles (167,820 kilometers) across, dwarfing the diameter of Earth several times over.

• Side Length: Each of the polygon's 10 straight edges measures roughly 10,425 miles (16,782 kilometers).

• Rotational Speed: The decagon is embedded within an eastward jet stream that circles Saturn at roughly 260 mph (420 km/h). However, the geometric wave structure itself drifts at a slow pace of roughly 6 mph (10 km/h), requiring nearly 800 Earth days to complete a single full rotation.

3.Why Was It Discovered Just Now?

For decades, astronomers actively looked for a southern polygon counterpart in observations from both Hubble and the Cassini spacecraft (which orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017), finding zero trace of regular geometric symmetry.

Two critical factors made this discovery possible:

• Saturn’s 29.5-Year Orbital Tilt: Because Saturn takes almost three decades to orbit the Sun, each season lasts around 7.5 Earth years. From 2012 to 2023, Saturn’s southern hemisphere was tilted away from Earth's line of sight, preventing direct optical observation of the south pole.

• Citizen Science Collaboration: As the south pole tipped back toward Earth, amateur astronomers Trevor Barry in Australia and Jean-Paul Oger in France noticed unusual wavy disturbances in 2024. Subsequent deep-stack polar projections and Hubble Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3) multi-filter datasets traced the emergence of the 10-sided boundary back to 2023.

4.Decagon vs. Hexagon: How Do They Compare?

While both structures represent atmospheric Rossby waves trapped inside high-velocity circumpolar jet streams, the two features exhibit distinct behavioral differences.

Unlike the northern hexagon, which has retained identical sharp contrast along all vertices since 1980, the decagon displays uneven, varying shades across its 10 segments. This indicates that astronomers are witnessing a giant planetary weather system in its formative stages.

5.The Prime Suspect: The 4,000-km Anticyclonic 'Red Spot'

What forced an atmospheric jet stream into a symmetrical 10-sided polygon?

Just north of the wave, astronomers identified an intense, high-pressure anticyclonic vortex spanning roughly 2,500 miles (4,000 km) across—a Saturnian analog to Jupiter’s Little Red Spots. Computer fluid-dynamic simulations show that perturbations from this massive vortex likely sheared the eastward wind stream, causing the jet to meander into harmonic polygonal wavelengths.

As Saturn’s southern hemisphere heads toward peak summer sunlight in 2032, increasing solar thermal radiation will either stabilize the decagon into a permanent fixture or disrupt its fragile balance, causing it to break apart.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1: What is the new shape found on Saturn?

Astronomers identified a 10-sided atmospheric wave, known as a decagon, encircling Saturn’s south pole at roughly 60 degrees south latitude.

Q2: How does the decagon compare in size to Earth?

The entire decagon spans over 104,000 miles across, meaning roughly 13 Earths could fit side-by-side inside its perimeter.

Q3: Is the decagon permanent?

Unlike the northern hexagon, which has persisted for over 44 years, the decagon was only detected in data from 2023 onward. Scientists are monitoring whether seasonal solar radiation will reinforce or dissolve the pattern.
