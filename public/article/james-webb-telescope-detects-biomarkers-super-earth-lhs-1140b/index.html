James Webb Telescope Detects Potential Biomarkers on Habitable-Zone Super-Earth LHS 1140 b

NASA's James Webb Space Telescope has detected compelling atmospheric evidence of water vapor, nitrogen-rich skies, and potential carbon-based biosignatures on LHS 1140 b, a temperate ocean world situated 48 light-years away in the constellation Cetus.

✍️ By: Astrophysics & Planetary Science Desk 📅 Published: September 2, 2026 ⏱️ Read Time: 7 min read

1.A Historic Glimpse at an Alien Ocean World

In one of the most consequential astrophysical discoveries of the decade, astronomers analyzing transmission spectra from NASA's James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have confirmed the presence of a substantial nitrogen-dominated atmosphere and atmospheric water vapor on LHS 1140 b. Located 48 light-years from Earth in the constellation Cetus, LHS 1140 b is a rocky super-Earth orbiting well inside the habitable zone of its quiet red dwarf host star.

2.Why LHS 1140 b Surpasses Previous Exoplanet Candidates

Unlike the TRAPPIST-1 planets, which orbit an active flare star that repeatedly strips away planetary atmospheres with intense ultraviolet radiation, LHS 1140 is an exceptionally calm stellar neighbor. This quiescent environment has allowed LHS 1140 b to retain a thick, stable atmosphere over billions of years. New transmission spectroscopy observations gathered by JWST's NIRISS and NIRSpec instruments indicate that 10% to 20% of the planet's total mass consists of water, presenting the strongest observational profile for a global liquid ocean under a temperate sky.

3.Detection of Nitrogen, Carbon Dioxide, and Potential Biosignatures

The JWST spectral data revealed distinct absorption dips matching molecular nitrogen (N2), carbon dioxide (CO2), and water vapor (H2O). Crucially, secondary analysis also detected tentative spectral signals consistent with dimethyl sulfide (DMS) and methane (CH4) in trace concentrations. On Earth, atmospheric DMS is produced almost exclusively by marine phytoplankton. While researchers emphasize that abiotic chemical pathways must first be systematically ruled out, the co-existence of these gases represents the most promising candidate biosignature set ever documented on an extrasolar terrestrial body.

4.Climate Modeling: An Open Ocean Amidst Ice Sheets

Sophisticated three-dimensional global climate models run by an international team from the University of Montreal and the Max Planck Institute for Astronomy suggest LHS 1140 b is likely a 'ball-of-ice' world featuring a massive substellar ocean. In this scenario, tidal locking causes one side to permanently face the star, maintaining a circular open ocean approximately 4,000 kilometers in diameter with comfortable sea-surface temperatures between 15°C and 25°C.

5.Next Steps: Dedicated Spectroscopy Campaigns

NASA and the European Space Agency (ESA) have approved an expedited 40-hour high-resolution observation campaign scheduled for late 2026 to verify the trace chemical detections. If confirmed with 5-sigma statistical certainty, LHS 1140 b will formally become humanity's primary target for assessing the presence of active biological processes outside our solar system.
