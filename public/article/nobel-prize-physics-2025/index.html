2025 Nobel Prize – Physics

Three scientists of British, French, and American nationality awarded the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics for groundbreaking discoveries in quantum mechanics and macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling.

✍️ By: Nobel Committee for Physics 📅 Published: October 7, 2025 ⏱️ Read Time: 5 min read

1.Prestigious Award Announcement

The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics has been awarded to three scientists of British, French, and American nationality for their exceptional discoveries in the field of quantum mechanics. The Nobel Committee announced this afternoon at the Nobel Prize ceremony in Stockholm, Sweden, that John Clarke, Michel Devoret, and John Martinis will receive this prestigious award.

2.Groundbreaking Discovery

The laureates are being recognized 'for the discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantization in an electrical circuit.' This revolutionary work has bridged the gap between the microscopic quantum world and macroscopic systems that we can observe and manipulate directly.

3.Quantum Properties at Scale

The Nobel Committee honored these laureates for demonstrating that 'the strange properties of the quantum world can be embodied in a system large enough to be held in one's hand.' This achievement represents a fundamental breakthrough in our understanding of how quantum mechanics can manifest in larger, more tangible systems.

4.Scientific Significance

Their work has shown that quantum mechanical effects, previously thought to be limited to atomic and subatomic scales, can be observed and controlled in electrical circuits of macroscopic size. This discovery has opened new possibilities for quantum computing, quantum sensing, and our fundamental understanding of the quantum-classical boundary.

5.Technological Applications

The practical implications of their discoveries extend to quantum computing technologies, where macroscopic quantum effects are harnessed to create quantum bits (qubits) that can exist in multiple states simultaneously. This work has been instrumental in the development of superconducting quantum computers and advanced quantum sensors.

6.Prize Distribution

Accordingly, the prize amount of 11 million Swedish kronor will be divided equally among the three scientists. This recognition celebrates not only their individual contributions but also the collaborative nature of advancing our understanding of quantum mechanics in macroscopic systems.

7.Historical Context

Last year, this prize was awarded to Geoffrey Hinton, known as the 'Godfather of AI,' and John Hopfield, for their fundamental discoveries in machine learning that paved the way for the development of artificial intelligence used today. This year's award continues the Nobel Committee's recognition of discoveries that bridge theoretical physics with practical technological applications.
