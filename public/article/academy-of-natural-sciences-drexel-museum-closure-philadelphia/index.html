Philadelphia’s Academy of Natural Sciences Museum to Close After Nearly 200 Years: Inside the Landmark Decision

Philadelphia's Academy of Natural Sciences will close its museum on Sept 30, 2026. Discover why Drexel University made the move and what happens to its 19M fossils.

✍️ By: Science & Museum Culture Editorial Desk 📅 Published: September 1, 2026 ⏱️ Read Time: 5 min read

1.A Somber Milestone for American Natural History

In a somber milestone for American natural history and scientific education, the museum at the Academy of Natural Sciences of Drexel University will officially close its doors to the public at the end of the business day on September 30, 2026. Its final day of regular public weekend operations will be Sunday, September 27.

Founded in 1812 by seven visionary naturalists, the Academy stands as the oldest natural sciences research institution in the Western Hemisphere, having welcomed generations of visitors and researchers to its Benjamin Franklin Parkway galleries since 1828.

The announcement, jointly issued by Drexel University President Antonio Merlo, PhD, and Academy Board of Trustees Chair Suzanne Biemiller, marks the end of a 198-year museum era while pivoting the institution toward safeguarding its vast global scientific research enterprise.

2.Why Is the Museum Closing? Economic Pressures and Shifting Attendance

The decision to shutter the public galleries stems from compounding post-pandemic financial deficits and long-term structural shifts in cultural visitor foot traffic:

• Post-Pandemic Foot Traffic Deficits: Despite significant investments in redesigned family galleries, experiential programming, and summer special exhibitions, public visitor attendance struggled to rebound to sustainable pre-pandemic levels.

• Escalating Facility & Overhead Costs: Operating the historic Benjamin Franklin Parkway complex placed mounting financial strain on institutional budgets, leading the museum to reduce public hours to three days per week (Friday through Sunday) in late 2025.

• Broader Drexel Financial Overhaul: Drexel University—which affiliated with the Academy in 2011—has been navigating multi-year deficit reduction strategies, academic calendar consolidations, and nationwide higher education enrollment shifts. Subsidizing the museum's annual operational deficits (surpassing $8.2 million in FY2025) became financially unsustainable.

Drexel confirmed that approximately 27 of the Academy's 80 staff members (roughly one-third of the workforce) will be directly impacted by the decommissioning of the museum galleries.

3.What Happens to the 19 Million Specimens and Research Centers?

While public galleries—including the celebrated Dinosaur Hall, live animal exhibits, and historic wildlife dioramas—will cease public admissions, the Academy’s core scientific research enterprise remains fully intact and operational:

• 19 Million Biological Specimens & Fossils: The scientific repository houses an irreplaceable global archive, including over 73,000 holotype type specimens used daily by international scientists to track biodiversity loss, species extinction, and environmental change over centuries.

• Historic Archives & Rare Books: The Academy’s Library and Archives—preserving more than 75,000 rare volumes dating back to 1529 alongside original collections from Thomas Jefferson and the Lewis & Clark Expedition—will continue serving academic scholars and historians.

• The Patrick Center for Environmental Research: Scientific research departments and watershed monitoring programs will continue operating within the Parkway facility, maintaining close ties with Drexel University’s Department of Biodiversity, Earth and Environmental Sciences (BEES).

4.Leadership Shakeup: CEO Dr. Scott Cooper Steps Down

Concurrent with the public closure announcement, Dr. Scott Cooper, who served as President and CEO of the Academy of Natural Sciences for nearly nine years, announced his immediate resignation.

During his tenure since 2017, Cooper spearheaded community participatory science initiatives, revitalized exhibition narratives like the "Botany of Nations," and advanced civic conservation initiatives such as Lights Out Philly to protect migratory birds from urban light pollution.

Cooper will remain with the institution in a temporary senior advisory capacity to help oversee the decommissioning process and ensure institutional continuity.

5.Mediation and the Search for a Sustainable Future

The long-term future of public access to the Academy’s historical collections remains under active community and civic discussion:

• Independent Facilitation: Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro and Philadelphia Mayor Cherelle L. Parker have engaged former Comcast executive and U.S. Ambassador David L. Cohen to act as an independent third-party facilitator between Drexel leadership, Academy trustees, and city stakeholders.

• Reimagined Public Engagement: Board Chair Suzanne Biemiller emphasized that leadership has not abandoned public engagement permanently, exploring possibilities for curated traveling exhibitions, regional educational partnerships, digital archives, and potentially a sustainable, reconfigured gallery footprint in the future.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1: When is the last day to visit the Academy of Natural Sciences museum?

The museum closes permanently on September 30, 2026, with its final regular public weekend operating day taking place on Sunday, September 27, 2026.

Q2: Are the Academy’s dinosaur fossils and collections being sold?

No. The entire 19-million-specimen collection, research laboratories, and rare book archives will be retained and curated on-site for scientific research and educational study.

Q3: Why did Drexel University close the museum?

The decision was driven by sharp post-pandemic drops in museum foot traffic, rising operational and building maintenance costs, and broader budgetary consolidation across Drexel University.
