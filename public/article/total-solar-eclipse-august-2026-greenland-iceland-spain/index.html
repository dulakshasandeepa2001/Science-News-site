Total Solar Eclipse August 2026 Crosses Greenland, Iceland, and Spain

A total solar eclipse on August 12, 2026 swept a narrow path of totality across Greenland, Iceland, and Spain, plunging areas into midday darkness while millions across Europe witnessed a spectacular partial eclipse.

✍️ By: Daily Science News 📅 Published: August 12, 2026 ⏱️ Read Time: 5 min read

1.Day Turns to Night Across Europe

On August 12, 2026, a total solar eclipse turned day into night across parts of the world. A narrow path of totality crossed Greenland, Iceland, and Spain, offering a rare and breathtaking view of one of nature's most spectacular phenomena. For observers fortunate enough to be in the path, the sky darkened dramatically, stars became visible in the daytime, and the air temperature dropped noticeably within minutes.

2.What Is a Total Solar Eclipse?

A total solar eclipse occurs when the Moon completely covers the Sun. During totality, the sky darkens dramatically and the temperature drops by several degrees. Observers along the path of totality also gain the rare opportunity to see the Sun's outer atmosphere — the corona — glowing in a halo of white light around the Moon's dark silhouette. This phenomenon is only visible from Earth during the brief window of totality and cannot be replicated by any other astronomical event.

3.Path of Totality: Greenland, Iceland, and Spain

Totality began over Greenland's Scoresby Sund region at around 4:35 PM local time, lasting approximately 1 minute and 46 seconds. In Reykjavik, Iceland's capital, the eclipse began around 5:48 PM, with totality lasting approximately 1 minute. In Spain, the eclipse reached the coastal city of Gijón at around 8:26 PM and Burgos at around 8:28 PM local time, offering spectacular views to thousands of observers who had gathered along the northern Spanish coast.

4.Millions Across Europe See a Partial Eclipse

Millions of people elsewhere in Europe, outside the narrow path of totality, were still treated to a remarkable partial eclipse. The Sun was 99.96% covered as seen from Madrid, Spain; 92% from Paris, France; 91% from London, United Kingdom; and 84% from Berlin, Germany. The near-total eclipse visible from Madrid made it one of the best views in Europe outside the path of totality, drawing huge crowds to parks and open spaces across the Spanish capital.

5.Safety Advice: Use Certified Solar Filter Glasses

Authorities across all affected countries strongly advised the public to use certified solar filter glasses when viewing the eclipse, and to fit solar filters onto any telescopes or binoculars used for observation. Looking directly at the Sun without proper protection, even during a partial eclipse, can cause permanent and severe retinal damage. Only during the brief moments of complete totality — when the Sun is fully covered — is it safe to view without filters.

6.Next Eclipse Visible from Sri Lanka: August 2027

For those in Sri Lanka who missed this spectacular event, the next eclipse visible from the island will occur on August 2, 2027 — a partial eclipse — followed by another partial eclipse on July 22, 2028. These events are eagerly anticipated by astronomy enthusiasts across South Asia, and sky-watching groups are already making preparations to ensure safe and educational public viewing events are organized well in advance.
