Amazing Paint Coating That Provides Water from Hot Air

A team of researchers from the University of Sydney has developed an advanced nanotechnology paint coating that can cool buildings while collecting water from the air through passive radiative cooling, offering a solution to heat and water scarcity worldwide.

✍️ By: Science & Technology Correspondent 📅 Published: May 12, 2026 ⏱️ Read Time: 5 min read

1.Revolutionary Nanotechnology Solution

A team of researchers from the University of Sydney in Australia has succeeded in providing an effective solution to the severe heat and water scarcity prevailing around the world. This was achieved by introducing an advanced nanotechnology paint coating that has the ability to cool buildings while collecting water from the air. This coating, introduced by Dewpoint Innovations, operates through a process called 'passive radiative cooling'.

2.How Passive Radiative Cooling Works

In the passive radiative cooling process, buildings are kept cool without consuming electricity by reflecting sunlight and releasing heat back into space. Research has confirmed that this coating can reflect up to 96% of solar energy. It has the capability to reduce heat by 30 degrees Celsius compared to a normal dark roof, and it has been found that this can reduce electricity consumption for home cooling by approximately 34%.

3.Combat Urban Heat Island Effect

Experts point out that this will be a great help in controlling the excessive heat in cities, known as the 'urban heat island effect'. The ability to cool buildings passively while maintaining structural integrity represents a significant breakthrough in sustainable urban design and energy efficiency. This technology could play a crucial role in making cities more livable as global temperatures continue to rise.

4.Water Collection Through Condensation

While this coating keeps buildings cool, water droplets form due to condensation of moisture in the air on the cool surface. Initial research has shown that a 200 square meter roof can collect approximately 74 liters of water per day. Researchers say this would be more effective in tropical countries like Singapore or the Amazon where humidity is above 70%.

5.Diverse Applications for Collected Water

The water collected in this way can be used for gardening activities as well as for drinking water after filtration and purification. In addition, researcher Ming Chiu says this technology can also be used to provide water for wildlife during droughts. This dual functionality of the coating makes it particularly valuable for regions facing both heat stress and water shortages.

6.Challenges and Future Outlook

However, it is noted that the price of this special type of paint can be 50% to 100% higher than ordinary paints. Attention continues to be focused on its durability over time and performance in cloudy weather conditions. Despite these challenges, Dewpoint Innovations company hopes that this technology will become an essential component in urban design and building planning in the future, helping to create more sustainable and resilient cities.
