Artemis III Astronauts Named

NASA announced a four-member crew for Artemis III — a high-risk 2027 crewed demonstration mission in Low Earth Orbit that will validate docking and landing procedures for future lunar landings.

✍️ By: NASA 📅 Published: June 10, 2026 ⏱️ Read Time: 5 min read

1.Washington Announcement and Mission Redesign

WASHINGTON — Marking the beginning of another new phase in space exploration, NASA officially announced the four-member crew selected for the Artemis III mission. Once planned as a lunar surface landing mission, Artemis III has been redesigned as an advanced crewed demonstration mission conducted in Low Earth Orbit to rehearse and verify the complex docking and transfer procedures required for future lunar surface missions.

2.Randy Bresnik — Mission Commander

Randy 'Comrade' Bresnik, a retired U.S. Marine Corps Colonel and veteran astronaut, will serve as mission commander. Selected by NASA in 2004, Bresnik previously commanded the International Space Station and brings extensive long-duration spaceflight and test-pilot experience expected to be invaluable during this high-risk validation mission.

3.Luca Parmitano — Mission Pilot

Representing the European Space Agency, Italian astronaut Luca Parmitano has been selected as mission pilot. Parmitano has flown two long-duration ISS missions and served as the first Italian ISS commander. A distinguished test pilot, he brings deep operational know-how in microgravity research and complex spacecraft operations.

4.Dr. Frank Rubio — Mission Specialist

U.S. Army Colonel and flight surgeon Dr. Frank Rubio joins the team as mission specialist. Rubio recently set the American single-flight duration record with 371 days aboard the ISS. His combined medical and flight experience will be critical for monitoring crew health and human-performance systems during extended orbital tests.

5.Andre Douglas — Mission Specialist

Systems engineer and U.S. Coast Guard Reserve officer Andre Douglas will serve as mission specialist. A member of the 2021 astronaut candidate class, Douglas has worked on marine robotics and planetary defense and contributed to NASA's DART mission — skills that will support complex systems integration and fault management during Orion operations and docking exercises.

6.Mission Objectives

Scheduled to launch in late 2027, Artemis III will operate as a high-risk validation mission similar in purpose to Apollo 9. Its primary goals are to demonstrate docking operations with commercial lunar landing systems, test flight and life‑support systems and communications in a real orbital environment, evaluate Axiom spacesuit readiness for future lunar EVAs, and refine life‑support and docking concepts through extended orbital stays. Successful completion will lay the groundwork for Artemis IV and future lunar surface operations.

7.The Bigger Picture

Artemis III highlights continued international and commercial collaboration in NASA's Moon program. By rehearsing complex transfers and validating new hardware and procedures in Earth orbit, the mission reduces risk for subsequent lunar landings and advances long-term goals including sustained lunar presence and eventual human missions to Mars.
